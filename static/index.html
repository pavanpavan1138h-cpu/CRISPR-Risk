About the Project

CRISPR-Risk is an advanced bioinformatics platform built to address one of the critical challenges in gene editing: Biosafety. While CRISPR-Cas9 is a powerful tool, inadvertent edits at off-target genomic sites can lead to unintended consequences. This tool predicts these risks to ensure safer experimental designs.

Methodology

Our approach combines machine learning with biological context to provide a holistic risk assessment:

  • Off-Target Prediction: We utilize a Random Forest Classifier trained on extensive synthetic datasets. The model analyzes sequence homology to identify potential off-target sites where the Cas9 nuclease might mistakenly cut.
  • Biosafety Risk Score (BRS): A novel scoring metric that goes beyond simple probability. The BRS integrates multiple biological factors to weigh the impact of an off-target edit:

    BRS = P × (0.3E + 0.2C + 0.2F + 0.1D + 0.2H)

    Where:
    • P (Probability): likelihood of cleavage at the specific site.
    • E (Essentiality): Is the gene critical for cell survival?
    • C (Chromatin): Is the DNA accessible (open chromatin) vs. tightly packed?
    • F (Function): Does the cut occur in a coding region (Exon) or non-coding (Intron)?
    • D (Distance): Proximity to the Transcription Start Site (TSS).
    • H (Disease): Is the gene associated with known diseases?

Future Roadmap

We aim to integrate real-time genomic databases (like Ensembl) and expand support for other Cas variants (Cas12a, Cas13) to make this a universal tool for gene editors.
